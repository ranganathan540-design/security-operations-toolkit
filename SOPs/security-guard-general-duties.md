# Security Guard – General Duties & Responsibilities SOP

## 1. Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) provides general guidelines for security guards performing duties at commercial, industrial, institutional, residential, healthcare, and other facilities.

The objective is to maintain safety, security, discipline, access control, and proper reporting while ensuring professional conduct.

## 2. Scope

This SOP provides general guidance for security personnel assigned to:

- Main gates and entrances
- Reception and visitor access points
- Industrial and commercial premises
- Offices and institutions
- Warehouses
- Hospitals and healthcare facilities
- Residential premises
- Parking and vehicle entry points
- Patrolling duties

Site-specific instructions and applicable laws always take priority over this general SOP.

## 3. Reporting for Duty

Security guards should:

- Report for duty on time.
- Wear the prescribed uniform properly.
- Maintain a neat and professional appearance.
- Carry required identification and authorized duty equipment.
- Receive instructions from the outgoing guard or supervisor.
- Check the occurrence book, logbook, and pending instructions.
- Understand any special instructions applicable to the shift.
- Confirm that communication equipment is operational where provided.

## 4. Shift Handover and Takeover

Before taking charge, the incoming guard should verify:

- Keys and access cards
- Communication equipment
- Security registers
- Pending visitor matters
- Expected visitors or contractors
- Material movements
- Vehicle movements
- Equipment status
- Previous incidents
- Special instructions

Any discrepancy should be immediately reported to the supervisor or authorized representative.

## 5. Access Control

Security guards should:

- Allow entry only according to the site's approved access policy.
- Verify identification where required.
- Check visitor authorization.
- Ensure visitors follow registration procedures.
- Issue visitor passes where applicable.
- Prevent unauthorized access to restricted areas.
- Never lend or misuse access cards, keys, or credentials.
- Immediately report suspicious or unauthorized entry attempts.

## 6. Visitor Management

When handling visitors:

1. Greet the visitor professionally.
2. Ask whom they wish to meet.
3. Verify the purpose of the visit.
4. Obtain authorization according to site procedure.
5. Record required visitor details.
6. Issue a visitor pass where applicable.
7. Explain relevant site safety and security requirements.
8. Ensure the visitor returns the pass before leaving where required.

Personal information should be collected and handled only as permitted by applicable policy and law.

## 7. Vehicle Entry and Exit

Where vehicle control forms part of the assignment, guards should:

- Verify vehicle authorization.
- Record vehicle details when required.
- Follow approved procedures for employee, visitor, contractor, and delivery vehicles.
- Direct vehicles to designated parking or loading areas.
- Keep emergency access routes clear.
- Report suspicious vehicles immediately.

Vehicle searches should only be conducted when authorized by site policy, applicable law, and established procedures.

## 8. Material Movement

Materials entering or leaving the premises should be handled according to the organization's approved procedure.

Security guards should:

- Verify authorized documents where required.
- Check relevant material gate passes.
- Record inward and outward movements.
- Escalate discrepancies before permitting movement.
- Never approve unauthorized removal of company property.

## 9. Patrolling Duties

During patrols, security personnel should observe:

- Doors and windows
- Gates and fencing
- Emergency exits
- Fire safety equipment
- Restricted areas
- Lighting conditions
- Suspicious persons or vehicles
- Unusual sounds or smells
- Water leakage
- Electrical hazards
- Obstructions
- Unsafe conditions

Important observations should be recorded and reported.

## 10. Key Control

Security personnel responsible for keys should:

- Maintain an authorized key register.
- Issue keys only to authorized persons.
- Record issue and return details.
- Never duplicate or transfer keys without authorization.
- Immediately report lost or missing keys.

## 11. Incident Reporting

All significant incidents should be reported promptly.

An incident report should normally record:

- Date and time
- Location
- Persons involved
- What happened
- Actions taken
- Persons informed
- Witness details, where applicable
- Relevant observations
- Follow-up required

Reports should contain factual observations rather than assumptions or unsupported conclusions.

## 12. Emergency Response

During an emergency:

1. Remain calm.
2. Assess the immediate situation without taking unnecessary risks.
3. Raise the alarm according to site procedure.
4. Inform the control room, supervisor, or authorized person.
5. Contact emergency services when required and authorized by procedure.
6. Assist evacuation according to the emergency plan.
7. Keep emergency access routes clear.
8. Direct emergency responders when necessary.
9. Record important details after the immediate danger has been addressed.

Protection of life takes priority over protection of property.

## 13. Fire Safety

Security guards should know, as applicable:

- Emergency contact procedures
- Fire alarm locations
- Emergency exits
- Assembly points
- Location and basic identification of fire extinguishers
- Site evacuation procedures

Firefighting equipment should only be used by persons who are trained and when it is safe to do so.

Never take unnecessary personal risks during a fire.

## 14. Professional Conduct

Security personnel should:

- Be polite, alert, and disciplined.
- Treat employees, visitors, contractors, and members of the public respectfully.
- Avoid arguments and unnecessary confrontation.
- Follow lawful and authorized instructions.
- Maintain confidentiality.
- Remain attentive while on duty.
- Avoid sleeping while on duty.
- Avoid unauthorized use of mobile phones.
- Never report for duty under the influence of alcohol or intoxicating substances.

## 15. Confidentiality

Security guards may become aware of sensitive information during their duties.

They should not disclose:

- Employee personal information
- Visitor information
- Security arrangements
- Access credentials
- CCTV information
- Internal incidents
- Client information
- Confidential documents

Information should only be shared with authorized persons for legitimate operational purposes.

## 16. Prohibited Actions

Security personnel should not:

- Leave the assigned post without proper relief or authorization.
- Permit unauthorized entry.
- Accept unauthorized gifts or favors connected with security decisions.
- Share passwords, keys, access cards, or restricted information.
- Falsify registers or reports.
- Use excessive or unauthorized force.
- Interfere with evidence at an incident scene except when necessary to protect life or prevent immediate danger.

## 17. End-of-Shift Handover

Before completing the shift:

- Update required registers.
- Record incidents and important observations.
- Hand over keys and equipment.
- Explain pending matters to the incoming guard.
- Communicate special instructions.
- Report unresolved issues to the supervisor.

The guard should leave the post only after completing the authorized handover process.

## 18. Supervisor Escalation

Immediately inform the supervisor or authorized representative about serious matters such as:

- Unauthorized entry
- Theft or suspected theft
- Fire
- Medical emergency
- Violence or threats
- Major safety hazards
- Missing keys or access credentials
- Suspicious packages or activities
- Significant property damage
- Serious policy violations

## 19. Important Note

This is a general-purpose open-source SOP template.

Organizations should adapt it according to:

- Site-specific risk assessments
- Client requirements
- Applicable laws and regulations
- Contractual requirements
- Emergency response plans
- Fire and occupational safety requirements
- Organizational policies

This document does not replace professional security, legal, regulatory, or emergency-response advice.
